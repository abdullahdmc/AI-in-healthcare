AI in healthcare research roundup — November 26, 2026

On the machine-learning model side, several new models targeted detection and risk stratification from routine data. A graph-based model (CLDD) trained on MIMIC-IV data from 61,191 patients and 2,000 diseases flagged multiple potential diagnoses per patient from EHR demographics and interaction history, without relying on medical tests. A separate AI system for low-dose CT lung cancer screening moved beyond size- and growth-based metrics like volume-doubling time, reportedly outperforming radiologists on nodule malignancy diagnosis across sizes and stages. Among risk-prediction work, a stroke cohort model reached an AUROC of 0.839-0.841 for 10-year cardiovascular mortality, a 480-patient heart-failure study built a nomogram for hospitalization-associated disability (41.88% incidence), and a COPD comorbidity nomogram reached an external validation AUC of about 0.90.

On LLMs in healthcare, the emphasis was on prompting discipline and evaluation rigor rather than new capability claims. A stroke-vignette study found a structured five-step prompt raised thrombolysis guideline adherence from 83.3% to 100% and eliminated unsafe recommendations (16.7% to 0%) for leading closed-source models, with smaller gains for open-source ones. Separately, researchers tested whether LLMs and reasoning models could draft clinical triage rule sets against a real COVID-19 decision-support ruleset, finding reasoning models performed better but still fell short of reliable reproduction. Two reviews added caution: one on human-evaluation reliability for healthcare LLMs, and one finding sparse, inconsistent safety and workflow-burden data for LLM-based medical report generation.

In agentic AI for medicine and health research, new work focused on safety layers and system architecture. CareGuardAI added a multi-agent controller that scores clinical-safety and hallucination risk before releasing patient-facing answers, beating baselines including GPT-4o-mini. Prompt-to-Pill extended multi-agent drug-discovery pipelines into clinical trial simulation and EHR use. A proposed seven-layer "compliance-first" architecture aimed to make hospital-wide agentic deployment auditable, while a simulated queue-triage system in high-volume outpatient clinics shifted critical-case counts from 13 to roughly 25 without hurting throughput. A new benchmark found autonomous "AI scientist" agents still trail human experts on biological discovery tasks (0.37 vs. 0.42).

Across all three areas, today's additions lean toward guardrails, benchmarking, and architecture over raw performance gains — a sign the field is maturing from proof-of-concept toward deployment-readiness questions.
