AI in healthcare research — a few things from today's reading

Today's LLM findings were largely about where language models help and where they don't yet match clinicians. GPT-4, validated against specialist consensus diagnoses for 101 patients with systemic autoimmune disease, reached 97.03% overall diagnostic accuracy — climbing from 82.18% on history alone once immunological results were added, and hitting 100% for lupus, Sjögren's syndrome, and three other conditions. In a head-to-head against real-world Medscape respondents on 1,426 case-challenge questions, GPT-4o and o1 scored 88.4% and 94.3% versus 85.0% for the human respondents. But context still matters: ChatGPT-4o's radiology accuracy swung from 19.9% on images alone to 80.9% once clinical data and answer options were added, and a 64-clinician evaluation found GPT-4 clearly ahead of GPT-3.5 on spinal neurosurgery and physical therapy scenarios. On the patient-facing side, a randomized trial of 1,297 parents found a brief GPT-4o chatbot boosted HPV vaccination intent as much as standard public health materials in the short term, but the effect faded by 45 days while the materials' effect held.

On the ML/DL side, today's papers clustered around ICU and cancer mortality prediction. Random forest and XGBoost models are now routinely matching or beating classic scores: random forest hit AUROC 0.8423 for general ICU mortality (vs 0.736 for SOFA), and a coronary artery disease model reached AUC 0.914 on external validation for 28-day mortality across nearly 16,000 patients. Random survival forest also outperformed Cox regression and XGBoost for both colorectal cancer disease-free survival (5-year AUC 0.799) and pulmonary large cell carcinoma overall survival (5-year AUC 0.863). Not every added-complexity model paid off, though — a machine learning mortality model for over a million pediatric surgery encounters performed no better than a simple existing regression score, a useful counterpoint to the pattern.

Together, today's studies reinforce a familiar theme: LLMs are creeping toward reliable diagnostic support in well-scoped clinical contexts, while ML risk models keep demonstrating strong discrimination in ICU and oncology settings — though not automatically better than simpler existing tools.
