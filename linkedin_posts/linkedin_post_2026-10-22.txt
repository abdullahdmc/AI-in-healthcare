AI in healthcare: today's research roundup

Several studies today look at how large language models perform against expert clinicians in time-pressured decisions. A retrospective study gave GPT-4 clinical data on 100 acute stroke patients and found its treatment recommendations agreed with real-world decisions at AUC 0.80 overall, rising to 0.94 for thrombectomy specifically, and with an expert stroke specialist at AUC 0.85. In the emergency department, ChatGPT-4o's triage calls on 60 case scenarios showed moderate-to-substantial agreement with four experienced physicians (kappa 0.695), with perfect sensitivity at the most urgent triage level but a drop to 50% sensitivity at a lower-urgency level. A feasibility study applied a HIPAA-secure GPT-4o to electronic health records to identify and summarize goals-of-care conversations in advanced cancer patients at scale, a task that otherwise requires labor-intensive manual chart review. A broader scoping review of 43 studies on AI agents in healthcare (conversational, workflow, and multimodal decision-support types) found most evaluation still happens in simulated or lab settings, with few real clinical pilots and rare measurement of safety outcomes.

On the prognostic and diagnostic side, a deep learning study tested whether genetic disease risk can be predicted from summary-level data (linkage disequilibrium matrices) instead of individual-level genotypes, finding most model architectures reached test error comparable to individual-level-data models. A systematic review and meta-analysis of 45 studies on machine learning for endometriosis diagnosis found clinical-feature models reached a pooled validation AUC of about 0.80, while genetic-information models scored much higher on training data (AUC ~0.98) but dropped substantially on validation. A VGG16-based convolutional neural network trained on over 6,300 ocular images plus clinical variables reached 90% accuracy for ocular disease diagnosis. And a wearable single-lead ECG study used an LSTM model to forecast 6-12 month NT-proBNP changes in persistent atrial fibrillation patients, reaching 82% directional accuracy for heart failure progression.

Across both groups, the recurring theme is a gap between promising discrimination metrics and validated real-world performance, whether that's an LLM's triage judgment under pressure or a diagnostic model's drop from training to validation data.
