AI in Healthcare: Research Roundup — September 28, 2026

On the LLM side, today's additions span clinical diagnosis, evaluation, and documentation. A randomized trial in nephrology had 97 clinicians from 9 countries complete 556 complex, open-ended case evaluations and found LLM-supported workflows improved diagnostic accuracy while AI-induced errors were rare. In clinical neuroanatomy, ChatGPT-4 showed stable diagnostic performance and precise anatomical terminology across 20 novel cases, while Gemini 2.5 was more sensitive to prompt phrasing. ClinicalGPT-R1, a reasoning-enhanced diagnostic LLM trained on 20,000 clinical records, outperformed GPT-4o on Chinese diagnostic tasks and matched GPT-4 in English. A four-way comparison of ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, and Copilot on multiple sclerosis questions found Gemini scored highest for relevance and transparency, with Copilot trailing. On documentation, a functional evaluation framework (DistillNote) found LLM summaries of clinical notes compressed 20x still retained 97% of the diagnostic signal (AUROC 0.92 vs. 0.94) for heart failure detection, while a qualitative pilot study of LLM-assisted SOAP notes in pediatric rehabilitation found friction came from workflow mismatch and clinician trust, not language quality. A new benchmark (BRIE) testing nine LLMs on EHR information retrieval found frequent omissions of clinically important information, especially when synthesis across multiple documents was required.

On the ML/DL side, risk and diagnostic models continued to post strong discrimination on disease-specific tasks. An AutoML model for trauma patients with hemorrhagic shock accurately predicted in-hospital mortality using GCS, ISS, lactate, and fibrinogen as top predictors. A lightweight LightGBM model for breast cancer risk, trained on routine lab panels from 13,285 patients, reached an internal AUC of 0.987 (external AUC 0.83) while running in under 6 seconds. A Vision Transformer outperformed CNN architectures for patient-level classification of acute pancreatitis on CT (89.19% accuracy, AUC 0.946), and a hierarchical multiscale fusion network combining CT and clinical variables achieved high precision for preoperative esophageal cancer T-staging across three centers. A systematic review of 26 studies found AI models for MASLD risk stratification consistently reached AUROC 0.72-0.95, outperforming conventional risk scores. Elsewhere, a knowledge-augmented multimodal EHR model combining an LLM with structured data reached AUCs of 0.84 and 0.92 for 30-day readmission and in-hospital mortality, respectively.

Taken together, today's findings reinforce a consistent pattern: LLMs are increasingly tested in realistic, open-ended clinical scenarios rather than multiple-choice benchmarks, with mixed but generally positive results, while ML/DL risk and diagnostic models continue to deliver strong, disease-specific discrimination that still needs broader external validation.
