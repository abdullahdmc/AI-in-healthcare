AI in Healthcare Research Roundup — November 4, 2026

Several new machine learning and deep learning models for diagnosis and prognosis surfaced this week. A hybrid CNN-LSTM-KNN-XGBoost framework (HyCARD-Net) targets cardiovascular disease diagnosis, while a Swin Transformer model reached 87.71% accuracy classifying eight skin lesion classes on ISIC-2019 under limited, imbalanced image data. A retrospective study of 55,253 patients at a Mexican tertiary care hospital found XGBoost best able to flag high in-hospital mortality risk from admission-time data, and a 452-patient study at Ankara Bilkent City Hospital showed machine learning can complement traditional statistics in identifying prognostic factors for stage III colon cancer. A review of heart failure predictive analytics, drawing on 3,270 screened publications, found random forest, SVM, and neural network models consistently useful for readmission and mortality risk stratification.

On LLMs in healthcare, new benchmarking work continues to separate technical capability from real-world readiness. MedHELM, a clinician-validated taxonomy spanning 121 tasks, found reasoning models like DeepSeek R1 (66% win-rate) leading, while LLMEval-Med built a 2,996-question benchmark from real EHR data with physician validation. A separate 36-case study found LLM clinical decision-making highly task-dependent, with near-perfect final-diagnosis accuracy but poor performance selecting diagnostic tests. On the applied side, NeuroBot, a retrieval-augmented chatbot for neurosurgery patient education, scored 5.70/6 on external expert review, and Mayo Clinic's MedEduChat raised prostate cancer patients' health confidence scores from 9.9 to 13.9 on a 16-point scale.

Agentic AI activity concentrated around clinical trials and drug discovery. An AI-assisted prescreening workflow for breast cancer trial eligibility reported 98.6% accuracy, cutting screening time from 20 minutes to 43 seconds per case, and a related system was evaluated in real-world multicenter deployment across 339 patient-trial pairs. A self-evolving agent system for redesigning failed trial protocols improved 83.3% of cases with a 5.7% average success-probability gain. In drug discovery, multi-agent systems (OrchestRA, DiscoVerse, CoDHy) coordinated target identification, molecule design, and hypothesis generation, while a new benchmark (AgentRx) found single-agent LLM systems outperform naive multi-agent ones, though a supervised baseline (AUROC 0.877) still beat all LLM agents on mortality prediction.

Across all three areas, today's studies pair strong reported performance with explicit caveats about generalization, calibration, or independent validation before clinical deployment.
