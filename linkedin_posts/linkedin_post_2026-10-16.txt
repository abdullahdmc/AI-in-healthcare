AI in Healthcare: Research Roundup — October 16, 2026

Today's LLM-related additions focused heavily on clinical decision support and workflow tasks rather than autonomous diagnosis. Google Research's SymptomAI, a Gemini Flash 2.0-based conversational agent, ran a national-scale study of 13,917 participants and produced differential diagnoses that independent clinicians judged more accurate than their own, with agents that conducted a dedicated symptom interview before diagnosing outperforming simpler user-guided chat. A JMIR study tested DeepSeek-R1, Gemini 2.5 Pro, and GPT-4o on liver, lung, and breast radiology reports, finding that adding progressively more clinical context produced only a modest numeric gain in diagnostic performance versus radiologists. Elsewhere, LLMs were put to narrower, supportive tasks: generating personalized patient question lists from EHR lab data, assisting endodontic symptom screening, and supporting oncology nursing decisions, with all three studies emphasizing the continued need for clinician oversight.

On the ML/DL side, a knowledge-augmented framework (KAMELEON) combined LLM-generated clinical reasoning with classical ML classifiers on MIMIC-III data, reaching AUROC of 0.85 for 30-day readmission and 0.92 for in-hospital mortality — and removing the LLM reasoning component dropped readmission AUROC to 0.70, highlighting how much of the gain came from the language-model layer. In imaging, a multitask EfficientNet-B0 model detected dementia presence (AUC 0.95) and staged its severity from brain MRI in one pass, while a deep learning model using MRI and transfer learning predicted neoadjuvant chemotherapy response and 5-year survival in adolescent osteosarcoma patients (C-index 0.831, small test set). Two prognostic/diagnostic comparisons rounded out the day: an XGBoost model outperformed Cox regression and random survival forests for prostate cancer survival prediction using SEER data, and an Extra Trees model screened for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease with AUC 0.879 internally and 0.822 on external Korean validation data.

Across both topics, the throughline is validation against real comparators — clinicians, radiologists, and classical statistical models — with external or held-out testing increasingly treated as a baseline expectation rather than an afterthought.
