AI in Healthcare: Research Roundup — October 15, 2026

Today's LLM-related additions centered on using language models as general-purpose infrastructure rather than narrow chatbots. An npj Digital Medicine study found that embeddings from general-purpose LLMs, produced by converting structured EHR codes into plain-language text, matched or exceeded a specialized EHR foundation model (CLMBR-T-Base) across 15 clinical prediction tasks, though at higher computational cost. On the patient-facing side, Verily Life Sciences published a predeployment validation of its LLM-based symptom-guidance tool, tested on 772 synthetic cases against five physicians' level-of-care judgments; the system kept overtriage within acceptable limits but missed its undertriage targets, underscoring the gap between simulation and real-world readiness. A JMIR Formative Research study tested a ChatGPT-based conversational version of the Beck Depression Inventory against the standard PHQ-9 in 115 participants, finding moderate correlation and diagnostic agreement.

On the ML/DL side, three disease-specific models reached notable discrimination. A Journal of Proteome Research study combined serum metabolomics with an XGBoost classifier to diagnose hepatocellular carcinoma, achieving an AUC of 0.876 internally and 0.813 in external validation, outperforming alpha-fetoprotein for early-stage detection. A Clinics (Sao Paulo) nomogram used LASSO-selected clinical variables to predict ICU sepsis mortality, reaching AUC 0.900 in training and 0.796 in validation. A Clinical Ophthalmology study trained an EfficientNet-B2 model with a weighted loss function to detect diabetic kidney disease from retinal images, aiming at a non-invasive screening alternative.

Taken together, today's additions reflect a continued split: LLMs are being validated as backbone infrastructure and triage support rather than diagnosis engines, while disease-specific ML/DL models keep pushing incremental gains in discrimination over existing clinical scores.
