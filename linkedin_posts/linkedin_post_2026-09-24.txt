AI in Healthcare: Research Roundup — September 24, 2026

On the LLM side, several new studies examined triage performance across specialties. A pilot study pitting four LLM chatbots against retina specialists on after-hours emergency calls found humans still more accurate overall (95% vs 76.7% diagnosis), though ChatGPT (90%) and Claude (83.3%) were statistically noninferior to human graders. A separate benchmarking study across five rare-disease datasets found LLM specialty-triage accuracy ranging widely from 0.44 to 0.71, while an evaluation of eight contemporary models on 48 clinical vignettes showed structured prompting lifting mean diagnostic accuracy from 89.84% to 91.67% and triage accuracy from 76.82% to 86.20%.

On broader evidence quality, a meta-research analysis of 11,628 PubMed records from 2023-2026 found medical LLM publications growing 45-fold, yet only 2.5% used randomized, controlled, or prospective designs, and the gap between a study's newest cited model and its own publication date widened from 1.33 to 6.08 quarters. A national survey of nearly 36,000 adults in China found moderate public acceptance of LLM-based healthcare (64.3/100), skewing lower among older adults and those with less digital-health experience. In a real-world multimodal evaluation across 539 inpatient cases, ten frontier LLMs all outperformed routine ward diagnoses on accuracy and safety scores.

On the ML/DL prognostic and diagnostic side, several nomogram studies validated risk-prediction tools: an invasive pulmonary aspergillosis model in ICU sepsis patients reached AUC 0.883/0.844 (training/validation), and a postherpetic neuralgia risk nomogram in herpes zoster patients reached AUC 0.758 with external validation. In imaging, a histology-based deep learning model for pituitary neuroendocrine tumors achieved AUC 0.912 for lineage classification across three independent cohorts, and a sequential-learning framework preserved diagnostic accuracy while adding prognostic capability for osteoarthritis, Alzheimer's disease, and breast cancer.

Across both threads, today's studies reinforce a consistent theme: LLMs are being pushed hardest on triage and diagnostic-reasoning tasks with mixed but improving results, while ML/DL prognostic and diagnostic models keep delivering well-validated, disease-specific risk scores ready for clinical use.
