AI in healthcare: daily research notes

On the prognostic and diagnostic modeling side, a new arXiv paper proposes bootstrapping-based regularization to reduce how much individual risk predictions shift between model refits, cutting instability by roughly two-thirds on the GUSTO-I and Framingham datasets while preserving discrimination. In applied work, a logistic regression model for acute kidney injury after cardiac arrest reached an AUC of 0.825 on external validation using MIMIC-IV data, and a meta-analysis pooling 68 studies on machine learning for poststroke mortality reported a pooled C-index of 0.727, alongside notable variability across individual models.

On the LLM side, a review in APL Bioengineering surveys ChatGPT's potential roles in precision medicine, from genetic sequence analysis to clinical trial recruitment support. A hybrid Bayesian-neural-LLM framework, DiagnoLLM, reached 88.0% accuracy on Alzheimer's disease detection while generating interpretable diagnostic reports. A randomized trial of 180 hospitalized patients found AI voice-cloned health education outperformed standard education on compliance and satisfaction, with content retention of 92.5% using patients' own cloned voice versus 86.7% for a physician's. A feasibility study of an LLM chatbot answering parental questions in the pediatric ICU found 99.3% of 1,225 generated sentences were accurate. Two new benchmarks target radiology documentation: one for longitudinal lung-finding timelines, another (PET-F2I) showing a fine-tuned 7B model outperforming 27 larger LLMs on PET/CT impression generation.

On agentic AI, a Johns Hopkins team's EligMeta framework narrowed 4,044 candidate gastric cancer trials to 39 relevant studies, recovering all 13 guideline-cited trials. DeepEvidence applies orchestrated agents to biomedical knowledge graphs across the discovery-to-evidence pipeline, while a separate paper proposes a governance framework for managing growing fleets of clinical AI agents. A CUHK/Stanford/Microsoft team's "Medical AI Scientist" reported higher first-run success rates than GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5-Pro on 57 self-reported research tasks.

Across all three areas, the throughline is the same: quantitative validation and governance tooling are maturing in parallel with capability claims, even as most agentic results remain self-reported and awaiting independent verification.
