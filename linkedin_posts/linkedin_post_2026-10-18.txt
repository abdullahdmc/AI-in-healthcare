AI in healthcare: today's research roundup

Two new benchmarks highlight a gap between LLM agents and real clinical workflows. PhysicianBench tested 13 proprietary and open-source agents on 100 long-horizon EHR tasks across 21 specialties; the top model reached only 46% pass@1, with open-source models topping out near 19%. A second benchmark, ART, found GPT-4o-mini and Claude 3.5 Sonnet achieved near-perfect data retrieval from EHRs after prompt refinement but accuracy dropped to 28-64% on aggregation tasks and 32-38% on threshold reasoning. Elsewhere, LLMs showed more promise in narrower documentation roles: Llama 3.3 extracted radiotherapy treatment sites from physician notes with about 94% accuracy, and a GPT-based conversational interface for depression screening (BDI-FS-GPT) correlated moderately with the standard PHQ-9 in a 115-person study.

On the machine learning side, a random-forest model (PRE-Screen-HCC) trained on over 900,000 UK Biobank participants outperformed existing hepatocellular carcinoma risk scores using only routine clinical data, validated externally in the All of Us cohort. An ECG-based deep learning ensemble for pre-echocardiography triage reached an AUROC of 0.87 internally and 0.84 across 20,053 external patients, though its precision-recall AUC fell from 0.66 to 0.50 outside the development site. A sepsis-prediction model told a cautionary story: an XGBoost model scored an AUROC of 0.82 in development but collapsed to 0.47 on external ICU data.

Together, these findings point to a familiar pattern: strong internal performance for both LLMs and traditional ML models, but meaningfully weaker results once tested on new agentic tasks or external populations.
