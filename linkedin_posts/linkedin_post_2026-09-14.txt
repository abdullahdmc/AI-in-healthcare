AI in healthcare research — a few things from today's reading

Today's LLM findings centered on diagnostic accuracy benchmarking across specialties, with mixed results. GPT-5 approached clinician-level sensitivity for STEMI diagnosis from 12-lead ECGs but was undermined by a very high false-positive rate, while GPT-4 with Vision reached 76% accuracy on neuroradiology board-style cases, well above prior GPT-4V radiology benchmarks (43%). ChatGPT-4o's ophthalmic diagnostic accuracy dropped from 80.1% with full clinical context to 54.7% on images alone, and four LLMs compared on 100 real otolaryngology cases scored 92-99% on diagnosis but far lower on treatment recommendations. A red-teaming study of 222 patient-posed medical questions found unsafe response rates of 5-13% across four major chatbots, while a smaller feasibility study found a GPT-4o-based PICU chatbot for parents was 99.3% accurate across 1,225 generated sentences.

ML/DL risk-prediction models again delivered strong, narrow discrimination on well-defined clinical questions. A stacked ensemble using just 10 physical-exam parameters predicted coronary heart disease risk with AUC 0.977 internally and 0.929 on external validation, and an XGBoost model predicted postherpetic neuralgia risk with AUC 0.826-0.840 across training and external cohorts. A next-generation model built on 382,589 patients and 3.9 million EHR encounters reached AUC 0.869 for 6- and 12-month acute myocardial infarction risk, and a UK Biobank study of over 500,000 participants produced an improved risk model for incident diabetic foot complications. In oncology, a deep learning model trained on routine pathology slides from 591 pancreatic cancer patients predicted postoperative recurrence using only histology images, with external validation on 302 additional patients.

The pattern from recent weeks continues: general-purpose LLMs perform unevenly on diagnostic tasks and struggle more with treatment decisions and safety than with pattern recognition, while purpose-built ML/DL models keep achieving high discrimination on specific, well-scoped prediction problems.
