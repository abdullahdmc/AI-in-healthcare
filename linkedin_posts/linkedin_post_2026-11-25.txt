AI in healthcare research roundup — November 25, 2026

On the machine-learning model side, several new risk and diagnostic models drew on underused data sources. A UK Biobank study of about 233,589 participants found that adding biochemical and metabolomic biomarkers to conventional risk factors improved prediction of incident chronic kidney disease and CKD-related mortality. A multicenter causal machine-learning tool trained on 789 patients predicted postcranioplasty complications with SHAP-based decision support for modifiable surgical variables. A meta-analysis of 13 studies (38,565 patients) found standalone deep learning reached AUC 0.89 for breast cancer detection on digital breast tomosynthesis, statistically indistinguishable from radiologists (AUC 0.88-0.90). Two EHR models also stood out: KAT-GNN reached AUROC 0.93 for coronary artery disease onset and 0.88-0.92 for in-hospital mortality, while BiPETE improved alcohol/substance-use-disorder risk prediction (AUPRC) by 34-50% over baselines in depression and PTSD cohorts.

On LLMs in healthcare, the focus was documentation quality and conversational reliability rather than raw capability. One study found LLM-generated clinical notes can introduce "note bloat" and "diagnostic overcall" that propagate into downstream LLM outputs, with rewritten edits often repeating diagnosis titles verbatim in ways real clinicians rarely do. A GPT-4o-based diagnostic chatbot with retrieval-augmented generation reported around 90% accuracy on its test setup.

In agentic AI for medicine and health research, systems pushed further into end-to-end, multi-step workflows. An agentic clinical-history-taking system (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite, Grok-3) captured relevant history elements with over 85% accuracy and F1 against physician benchmarks, though diagnostic performance derived from those histories remained moderate. MedCaseAgent, a planner-writer-editor pipeline, retrieved the exact source article for 84.6% of rare-disease cases (versus 46.9% for the strongest baseline) when generating case reports, and clinicians in blinded review often preferred its reports to the originals. Vibe Medicine proposed a three-layer, skill-augmented agent framework for human-directed biomedical workflows spanning rare-disease diagnosis, drug repurposing, and clinical trial design.

Across all three areas, today's additions share a theme: narrower validation against human benchmarks (radiologists, physicians, expert reviewers) rather than standalone capability claims.
