AI in Healthcare: Research Roundup — October 13, 2026

Today's LLM-related additions centered on clinical documentation and diagnosis support. A JMIR Medical Informatics study fine-tuned Mistral, Meditron, and Llama models with parameter-efficient methods on 1,700 doctor-patient conversations to generate clinical notes; physician reviewers rated Llama3-8B and Mistral-7B as the most clinically coherent and accurate. A related BioNLP 2026 paper evaluated LLM-rephrased synthetic clinical notes at million-note scale, finding they preserve core clinical information for coarse-grained tasks but lose detail needed for fine-grained work like ICD coding. On the diagnosis side, a method called MERA paired LLM knowledge with contrastive ranking over disease candidates, reporting state-of-the-art diagnosis prediction on MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV.

Separately, an npj Digital Medicine study tested several LLMs on secure patient portal messages from diabetes patients to screen for depression and anxiety symptoms. Three of five models topped 90% on F1 and accuracy, with Llama 3.1 405B reaching 93% on both using zero-shot prompting alone.

The ML/DL additions were dominated by systematic reviews taking stock of existing models. A meta-analysis of prostate cancer prediction models pooled results across 85 studies, reporting AUCs of 0.79-0.85 across survival, recurrence, and treatment-response endpoints. A separate review of 13 COPD exacerbation and readmission studies (sample sizes 110 to 113,786) found most models at moderate-to-high risk of bias and rarely externally validated. A broader review of ML models for hospital readmission, length of stay, and mortality flagged limited calibration reporting as a recurring gap across the field. On individual models, a Communications Medicine study built ElasticNet models predicting 12-month cognitive and functional decline in Alzheimer's disease and MCI from routinely collected data, externally validated on 741 ADNI trajectories with a cognition mean absolute error of 2.19.

Across both topics, today's crop leans toward evaluation and synthesis — benchmarking LLM note quality and pooling evidence on existing ML risk models — rather than introducing dramatically new capability.
