AI in Healthcare: Research Roundup — October 7, 2026

Today's LLM additions lean toward patient-facing deployment and evaluation. A randomized controlled trial of PreA, an LLM chatbot handling history-taking and referral generation ahead of specialist visits, cut physician consultation time by 28.7% across 2,069 patients, with outcomes holding up even when the chatbot ran without staff support. In hospital documentation, a Gemini 2.5 Pro-based agentic workflow (MedAgentBrief) generated nightly discharge summaries across 384 admissions; physicians used its output in 57% of cases, rated 88% of unedited drafts as having no harm potential, and reported a significant drop in burnout scores. Elsewhere, CataractBot's expert-verified answers for cataract surgery patients were rated 84.5% accurate and complete, while scoping reviews flagged how early this space still is: only 8 studies exist on multiturn LLM chatbots for cancer patients and caregivers, and a separate review on LLM-based mental health chatbots found safety and privacy risks remain under-studied relative to the pace of deployment.

On the ML/DL side, prognostic nomograms kept targeting high-stakes, lower-profile conditions. A nomogram for acute chlorfenapyr poisoning hit a C-index of 0.988 in training (0.849 on validation) using procalcitonin, ALT, and dose as key predictors, while a readmission-risk nomogram for acute pancreatitis reached AUC 0.836 internally but dropped to 0.704 on external validation — a reminder that generalization remains the harder test. Imaging-based diagnostic models also advanced: a CBAM-ResNet model for Alzheimer's diagnosis reached AUC 0.876, a CNN classifying cervical CT scans distinguished Kikuchi disease, lymphoma, lymphadenitis, and tuberculosis with PR-AUC between 0.73 and 0.92, and an international reader study pitted a market-approved CNN against 96 dermatologists on real-world skin lesion classification.

Across both tracks, the same pattern holds: LLM tools are moving into live clinical workflows with measurable efficiency and burnout gains, while ML/DL diagnostic and prognostic models keep posting strong internal performance but a recurring external-validation gap.
